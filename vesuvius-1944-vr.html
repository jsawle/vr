How this works

Where the lava goes comes from the same method as the Vesuvius lava page (DOWNFLOW, Favalli and colleagues 2005), run on today's ground for the two 1944 flows: north (stopping at about 140 m above sea level) and south-east (about 350 m). Lava is drawn where at least 1 in 5 of the simulated flows went.

When it gets there is an estimate. The lava moves at a steady speed, set so that the northern flow reaches San Sebastiano early on 21 March, as recorded. The real speed varied from 50 to 300 m an hour. The colours show lava cooling from yellow-hot to a dark crust, and are for illustration.

The eruption column follows INGV's account: lava fountains throwing material up to about 4 km (21–22 March), then explosions with a column more than 5 km high (22–23 March). Studies estimate over 10 km at the peak. The wind carried ash 15–20 km east. Smaller explosions blew ash to the south-west until 29 March. The particles, fountain height and the shape of the grey ash on the ground are illustrations that follow those directions and distances.

Town labels are placed approximately. Heights are exaggerated 1.5 times, and the column is drawn to the same scale.

Sources: INGV Osservatorio Vesuviano · Geological Magazine reconstruction · 1944 lava flow map (Journal of Maps)
